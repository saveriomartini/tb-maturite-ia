# 4.4 Organizational Strategy Dimension

The Organizational Strategy dimension examines how effectively an
organization positions itself to deliver business value through AI for the
selected organizational scope
--------

## 4.4.1 AI Strategy Development Capability Area

Example Artifacts
• AI strategy documentation
• section of a larger
organizational strategy which
includes AI adoption strategy,
mission and vision documents
• documentation of business
goals that will be measurably
improved with AI
• communication plan
• examples of internal
newsletters communicating AI
adoption related progress
• project plan of AI adoption with
milestones
• value stream mapping
--------

## 4.4.2 AI Partnership Development Capability Area

Example Artifacts
• market research documents,
such as competitive
assessment
• market analysis
• trend analysis
• evaluation matrix
• gap analysis
• AI-relevant checklists
for contracts
---------

## 4.4.3 Organizational Structure Calibration Capability Area

Example Artifacts
• documents which share
guidance that defines an
organization’s core purpose
and long-term aspirations—
what they expect to achieve
and how progress will
be measured
• organizational structure which
calls out AI adoption roles
• AI strategy
• 360 Framework
• enumeration of core functions
leading to AI adoption
---------

## 4.4.4 Future Ready Planning Capability Area

Example Artifacts
• predictive analysis
• future planning measurement
framework using baseline or
benchmark performance
• future scenarios
• collaboration exemplars and
case studies
----------

## 4.4.5 Capability Areas Supporting the Organizational Strategy Dimension

Capability areas within the Organizational Strategy dimension span
multiple maturity levels, reflecting the need for the organization’s
AI adoption strategy to evolve in sophistication, alignment, and scope as
overall maturity increases. In addition, the following capability areas are
closely related to Organizational Strategy:

• Refer to Workforce and Culture: Organizational Culture
Evolution for goals and practices related to developing
organizational capabilities.
• Refer to Technology Ecosystem: Supply Chain Management for
goals and practices related to managing and maintaining existing
third-party dependencies for vendor partners.
• Refer to Risk and Governance: Risk Management for goals and
practices related to overall risk management around additional
partnerships and procurement risks.

# 4.5 Workforce and Culture Dimension

The Workforce and Culture dimension focuses on capability areas
required to build an AI-literate organizational by fostering an
open-minded culture and providing the training and skills needed to
effectively integrate, develop, and use AI solutions
----------

## 4.5.1 AI-Literate Workforce Development Capability Area

Example Artifacts
• knowledge, skills, and abilities assessments
• role and job family descriptions
• skills surveys analysis
•skills taxonomy or framework
• workforce assessment and gap analysis
• training documents and guidelines
• course outlines; training materials (training materials on the responsible use of AI)
----------

## 4.5.2 Organizational Culture Evolution Capability Area

Example Artifacts
• culture, skills, behaviors, and mindsets documentation
• cultural adoption metric
• AI adoption stakeholder communication artifacts (newsletters, town halls, external resources made available to the workforce)
----------

## 4.5.3 Capability Areas Supporting the Workforce and Culture Dimension

Given the realities of AI infiltration to all aspects of system and product development as well as business operations, the AI-Literate Workforce Development capability area is expected to be accomplished at the Exploratory AI maturity level, while the Organizational Culture Evolution capability area will evolve as AI adoption maturity reaches Aligned AI level, closely related to AI Strategy Development. In addition, the following capability areas are also closely related to Workforce and Culture.

• Refer to Organizational Strategy: AI Strategy Development and Organizational Structure Calibration for goals and practices related to the AI adoption direction which drive Workforce and Culture.

# 4.6 Workflow Re-Engineering Dimension

The Workflow Re-Engineering dimension focuses on analyzing existing business processes for potential improvements, innovations, and recommendations of new workflows. AI can be used to transform key business practices, to assist and augment people's work, and to support business goals with sound measurement and analysis practices.
--------

## 4.6.1 Experimentation Capability Area

Example Artifacts
• experiment plans
• use case and workflow experimentation management dashboards
• resource plans for experimentation
• user experience (UX) and human-computer interaction (HCI) artifacts
• sandbox reference documents
• experiment results
• recommendations
• close out artifacts
--------

## 4.6.2 Business Workflow Innovation Capability Area

Example Artifacts
• business impact analysis (BIA)
• service-level agreement (SLA)
• recovery time objective (RTO)
• ROI analysis
• adoption rate
• business process documentation
• use case diagrams
--------

## 4.6.3 Human-in-the-Lead Automation Capability Area

Example Artifacts
• test plans
• subject matter expert (SME) assessments
• usability test findings and recommendations
• UX study plans and insights
• critical decision point analysis documentation with workflows
• tests of workflow execution based on AI risks
--------

## 4.6.4 Measurement and Analysis Capability Area

Example Artifacts
• benchmark and baseline performance
• dashboard with live tracking KPIs
• AI model performance (e.g., accuracy, F1 scores, speed/latency/throughput, response and recovery time)
• objectives and key results (OKRs)
• predictive analysis--success and failures
• rate of adoption
• reports to board members and shareholders
• stories about success and change
• template for test design and reporting
• test plan and expectations
• communication strategy and planning
• ROI analysis
• service continuity process
• service-level agreements
--------

## 4.6.5 Capability Areas Supporting the Workflow Re-Engineering Dimension

The Workflow Re-Engineering capability areas need to be established for the Aligned AI level of maturity, except for Experimentation which is essential for the Exploratory AI level of maturity. In addition, the following capability areas are also closely related to Workflow Re-Engineering.

• Refer to Risk and Governance: Risk Management for goals and practices related to overall risk management around additional security and workflow risks.
• Refer to Data: Data Lifecycle Management and Data Quality Assurance for goals and practices related to structured and unstructured data to support workflows, especially when agentic approaches are used.
• Refer to Engineering: AI Architecting, Test and Evaluation, and Transparency and Explainability related to goals and practices for designing and implementing the workflows with sound engineering principles.
• Refer to the Technology Ecosystem: Supply Chain Management for goals and practices related to managing and maintaining existing third-party dependencies.

# 4.7 Risk and Governance Dimension

The Risk and Governance dimension capability areas ensure that risks related to AI adoption are managed, AI adoption is executed with appropriate security practices, the relevant policies and standards are followed, and that the organization complies with regulatory obligations that are relevant to AI.
--------

## 4.7.1 Risk Management Capability Area

Example Artifacts
• risk score
• hazard and harms identification methods
• risk assessment approaches (e.g., root cause analysis, System Theoretic Process Analysis [STPA])
• audit reports of third-party AI partners
• EGRC strategy
• risk appetite documentation
• partners risk appetite documentation
• AI risk categories
--------

## 4.7.2 Policy and Compliance Capability Area

Example Artifacts
• legal playbook
• licenses and ownership of data and AI models
• local, state, federal, and international AI laws
• AI partner agreements and contracts
• organizational policies (e.g., privacy policy, HR policy)
• sector-related AI policies (e.g., automotive, health care)
• policy change log
--------

## 4.7.3 Responsible AI Capability Area

Example Artifacts
• responsible AI use guidelines
• hazard and harms identification methods
• organizational responsible AI risk policy
• responsible AI training
• responsible AI design and implementation guidelines
--------

## 4.7.4 Capability Areas Supporting the Risk and Governance Dimension

Given the increased categories of risks and uncertainty that AI introduces, all the capability areas of the Risk and Governance dimension are expected to be institutionalized at the Implemented AI level of maturity, even when the AI adoption scope is limited. In addition, the following capability areas are closely related and support Risk and Governance.

• Refer to Engineering: Test and Evaluation for goals and practices related to testing systems for bias.
• Refer to Operations: AI Model and Agent Security for goals and practices related to securing access to data, resources, and functionalities.

# 4.8 Data Dimension

The Data dimension focuses on maintaining relevant, high-quality, and secure structured and unstructured data to ensure data confidentiality, integrity, and availability. The capability areas of this dimension provide timely and relevant data for experimentation, development, training, test, and evaluation to strengthen the value and alignment of AI initiatives.
--------

## 4.8.1 Data Lifecycle Management Capability Area

Example Artifacts
• data curation guidance
• data cards
• data schema
• dataset description
• data ontology
• data lake criteria
• copyright guidance
• dataset classification guidance
• data usage guidance
• documents marked with classification
• data responsibilities
• data retention policy
• metadata
--------

## 4.8.2 Data Quality Assurance Capability Area

Example Artifacts
• data labeling guidance
• exploratory data analysis (EDA) outcomes
• extract, transform, load (ETL) pipelines
• bias identification and removal approaches
• metadata
--------

## 4.8.3 Capability Areas Supporting the Data Dimension

Data is essential to the success of any AI initiative, therefore, the capability areas of the Data dimension all need to be accomplished for Implemented AI level of maturity. In addition, the following capability areas support Data related practices.

• Refer to Risk and Governance: Responsible AI for goals and practices related to use of privacy and biases in data.
• Refer to Engineering: AI Architecting for goals and practices related to data architecture and data storage.
• Refer to Engineering: Legacy Integration for goals and practices related to system specific data errors, and Test and Evaluation for goals and practices related to use of data for test and evaluation.
• Refer to Operations: Monitoring for goals and practices related to data monitoring and collection from operational settings.

# 4.10 Operations Dimension

The Operations dimension brings together capability areas essential for monitoring systems for success, establishing feedback loops that identify future improvements, and managing AI deployments with an emphasis on AI model and agent management and security. These capability areas establish the backbone of effective long-term sustainment and evolution through the collection of relevant, actionable data for continuous improvement.
--------

## 4.10.1 AI Model Management Capability Area

Example Artifacts
• repository describing available AI models (including ML models, generative AI models, and vendor solutions models as applicable)
• model cards
• model descriptions
• model responsibilities
• model usage guidance
• licenses and ownership guidelines of models
• privacy and use policies
• AI model requirements
--------

## 4.10.2 AI Model and Agent Security Capability Area

Example Artifacts
• identity access management guidelines
• AI model and agent repositories
• agent interaction logs
--------

## 4.10.3 Monitoring Capability Area

Example Artifacts
• service level agreements
• monitoring logs
• alerts descriptions
• monitoring tools and implementations
--------

## 4.10.4 Capability Areas Supporting the Operations Dimension

AI Model Management, AI Model and Agent Security, and Monitoring are capability areas that are essential for any AI solution, and today any savvy organization which relies on software needs to have these as fundamental capabilities. Therefore, with the exception of Monitoring, they are expected at the Implemented AI level of maturity. Monitoring, is deferred to the Aligned AI level of maturity to give organizations an opportunity to determine which signals and decisions warrant monitoring to avoid supporting investments that may not pan out as helpful.

Other capability areas that support Operations include the following.

• Refer to Risk and Governance: Risk Management for goals and practices related to identification of risks.
• Refer to Data: Data Lifecycle Management and Data Quality Assurance for goals and practices related to acquiring high quality data from operational environments.
• Refer to Technology Ecosystem: Supply Chain Management for goals and practices related to third-party models acquired and maintained through supplier agreements.

# 4.11 Technology Ecosystem Dimension

The Technology Ecosystem dimension focuses on the organizational unit's capabilities to manage the AI-relevant dependencies, technologies, and required infrastructure to ensure the efficiency and scalability of AI solutions.
--------

## 4.11.1 Technology Infrastructure Capability Area

Example Artifacts
• deployment views of architecture descriptions
• AI strategy mapping for technology requirements
• capacity plans
--------

## 4.11.2 Supply Chain Management Capability Area

Example Artifacts
• demand and capacity planning documents
• standard operating procedures
• software bill of materials (SBOMs)
• compliance checklists
• AI model lineage
• dependency registries
• supplier API specifications
• transaction logs
• traceability and provenance records
--------

## 4.11.3 Deployment at Scale Capability Area

Example Artifacts
• scalability requirements
• technology, project management, and resources
• planning processes supporting scaled AI solutions
• duplicative solution lists
--------

## 4.11.4 Capability Areas Supporting the Technology Ecosystem Dimension

The Technology Ecosystem evolves as the maturity increases and the corresponding needs expand. The capability areas in this dimension reflect that progression by spanning multiple maturity levels from Implemented AI to Scaled AI.

Other capability areas that support Technology Ecosystem include the following.

• Refer to Organizational Strategy: AI Partnership Development for goals and practices related to selecting strategic partners in alignment with AI initiatives.
• Refer to Engineering: AI Architecting for goals and practices related to architectural concerns such as integration and system decomposition.
• Refer to Operations: Monitoring for goals and practices related to monitoring and feedback collection practices.
